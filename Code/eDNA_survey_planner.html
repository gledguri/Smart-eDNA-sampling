How the simulation and bounds work

We simulate natural-log concentration around log(100), with signal SD 1 and observation-noise SD 0.15. A 256-feature approximation to a squared-exponential Gaussian process uses the selected ρ in km. Reconstruction uses the squared-exponential covariance and the known simulation parameters. Concentrations are exp(log concentration), in illustrative copies/L.

The orange line is the posterior median. Lower and upper bounds are exp(m − 1.96s) and exp(m + 1.96s), where m and s describe the latent log-concentration posterior. These are pointwise 95% credible intervals conditional on the model, not bounds for new noisy observations or simultaneous coverage of the whole curve. Parameter uncertainty is omitted. ε is a planning RMSE, not the width of this band. Gaussian-process regression method.

Changing ε retains the field and a nested set of random locations, making comparisons easier. New-field and reshuffle buttons change separate random seeds. For browser responsiveness, plans above 300 samples are explicitly shown as a 300-sample demonstration.

How to read the result

—

If every station supplies every stratum, the required station count equals the samples per stratum. The total shown above counts all site-stratum observations. Filters, PCR replicates, and repeated visits are separate effort types.

The equation used is log(ε) = ω + β log(E) + θ log(ρ) + γ log(E) log(ρ). Its original support is ρ = 25–1,000 km and E = 3.58–20 samples per 10,000 km² per stratum. Calculations outside the E range extend the fitted formula and carry an extrapolation warning.
