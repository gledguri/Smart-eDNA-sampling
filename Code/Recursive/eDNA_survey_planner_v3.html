Visualization · Simulate & reconstruct

A survey in two dimensions

Simulate a GP reference surface, then reconstruct it using the equation’s recommended survey. Compare both surfaces on a shared 5 × 5 km grid, at approximately 10× the simulated-observation density. All maps use one depth.

Default ln(100), rounded to two decimals.

R simulator covariance: K = α exp(−d² / (2ρ²)); α is the spatial variance. All three surfaces use a shared 5 × 5 km evaluation grid (25 km² cells), approximately 10× the simulated-observation density.

Prediction error ε: at each location, ε = √((inferred log concentration − reference log concentration)²). Across the domain, ε = √(Σ (inferred − reference)² / M), where M is the number of evaluation points on the 5 × 5 km grid. Smaller ε means closer predictions. Sampling effort E is the separate sample-density measure.

ρ and target ε come from the planner above. Simulate chooses a fresh random seed each time. Changing ε or reshuffling samples keeps the reference field fixed. H changes only total N; the maps show one depth.

Equations, evaluation, and interpretation

N = ceil(40 A / 10,000) reference points are uniform within the drawn polygon. Survey size is n = ceil(E A / 10,000), where E = exp((ln ε − ω − θ ln ρ) / (β + γ ln ρ)).

The exact dense Gaussian draw is z ~ Normal(μ, K + σ²I). Reconstruction follows GP.stan: ẑ = μ + K(X, Xₛ)[K(Xₛ, Xₛ) + σ²I]⁻¹(zₛ − μ). Parameters are held at the entered values; they are not estimated by MCMC. A tiny diagonal jitter stabilizes the factorization.

The reference surface is the conditional GP mean using all N simulated observations. The inferred surface uses only the n selected observations. Both are evaluated directly from their GP equations at every 5 × 5 km grid center inside the drawn domain. This is approximately 10× the simulated-observation density (40 simulated observations vs. about 400 evaluation points per 10,000 km²). These are new evaluation locations, not additional survey samples; edge cells are clipped to the domain and centers outside it are excluded from ε.

At each dense location, rᵢ = inferred_surfaceᵢ − reference_surfaceᵢ; the ε surface and histogram contain √(rᵢ²). This is mathematically equal to |rᵢ|. Overall ε = √(Σ rᵢ² / M). The CSV includes all simulated observations and dense comparisons. used_for_inference is true for the survey samples shown in Figure 2 and false otherwise. Only rows with used_to_estimate_epsilon = true contribute to measured ε and the histogram.

This evaluates recovery of a full-data reference map, not latent-truth accuracy or independent held-out observations. The σ noise is in the simulated observations; both GP surfaces smooth that noise. Consequently these surface ε values differ from the point-to-noisy-observation ε values and have no automatic σ-sized floor. The measured result is never forced to match ε.

The manuscript equation depends on ρ and ε, not μ, α or σ. Changing GP variance assumptions therefore changes measured ε without recalibrating the recommendation. Each concentration map uses its own surface minimum and maximum in ln(copies/L), so equal colors across the two maps need not mean equal concentrations. Click a white-outlined point to see observed concentration in copies/L and the fitted values. Click anywhere on a surface to inspect the nearest evaluation point. Colored surface cells show direct GP evaluations, clipped to the domain; the ε surface uses every dense pointwise comparison.

Area and coordinates use a local spherical Lambert azimuthal equal-area projection (Earth radius 6,371.0088 km). Polygon area approximates the geographic boundary using its projected vertices; regional GP distances are planar km. Domains must span at most 2,000 km and stay between 80°S and 80°N. Up to 2,000 field points (500,000 km²) are supported for exact browser computation. Larger domains are rejected rather than reducing the fixed field density.

How to read the result

—

If every station supplies every depth, the required station count equals the samples per depth. The total shown above counts all site-depth observations. Filters, PCR replicates, and repeated visits are separate effort types.

The equation used is log(ε) = ω + β log(E) + θ log(ρ) + γ log(E) log(ρ). Its fitted support is ρ = 25–1,000 km and E = 1.5–20 samples per 10,000 km² per depth. Calculations outside the E range extend the fitted formula and carry an extrapolation warning.

Interpretation. The bounds are exp(log(C) − ε) and exp(log(C) + ε), assuming the true concentration was at 100 copies/L. The curve assumes normally distributed error in log space, producing a right-skewed lognormal density in copies/L.
